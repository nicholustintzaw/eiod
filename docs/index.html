eiod

Intake dietary environmental impact metrics, as Stata commands.

Tables 3, 4a–4g, 5a and 5b of Methods for Assessing Healthy, Environmentally Sustainable Diets Globally (Intake, 2026), implemented as plain do-files.

Three things to know before reporting any result

  1. HESD and HDLEI are nested, not independent. The HDLEI environmental cut-off is uniformly 1.52× stricter, so every HDLEI diet is also a HESD diet. One cell of the 2×2 is structurally empty.
  2. A low relative band is not evidence of sustainability. For greenhouse gases even band 1 exceeds the planetary boundary; for water even band 10 sits below it. The two metric families answer different questions.
  3. Eutrophication is printed in two different units — kg in Table 3, grams in Table 4f. Supply your data in grams, as the EIOF database gives it to you. This reading is an inference and has not been confirmed by Intake — see the reference dataset, section 2.

What this is not

It does not contain the EIOF database. It ships the published metric cut-offs and applies them to impact values you have already derived. Matching food items to impact factors, applying the edible-portion, processing and cooking-yield adjustments, and summing to the respondent-day is upstream work this package starts after.

It also does not calculate the Global Diet Quality Score — the composite metrics take a GDQS value as an input.
